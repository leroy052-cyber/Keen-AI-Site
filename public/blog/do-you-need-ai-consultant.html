Do You Actually Need an AI Consultant?

I'm an AI consultant, and I'm about to talk you out of hiring one. Not because we're not useful — but because plenty of businesses hire consultants when they really just need a YouTube tutorial and a free afternoon. Here's how to figure out which camp you're in.

You can probably sort it yourself if...

  • You need one specific tool. If you just need a chatbot for your website, an AI writing assistant, or an automated scheduler — you don't need a consultant. You need to pick a tool and set it up. There are excellent tutorials for all of these.
  • Your problem is really an organisation problem. If your "AI problem" is actually "we don't have a system for tracking customer enquiries," the answer might be a $30/month CRM, not a $10,000 AI project.
  • You're exploring, not implementing. If you want to understand what AI can do for your industry, start with reading, webinars, and free trials. A consultant is for when you know what you want to build and need help building it.

You probably need professional help if...

  • You need to connect multiple systems. If the solution involves pulling data from your CRM, pushing it through an AI model, and feeding results into your accounting software — that's integration work that benefits from experience.
  • You've tried and it didn't work. If you've spent three weekends on it and you're more confused than when you started, the cost of a consultant is less than the cost of your time going in circles.
  • The stakes are high. If getting it wrong means losing customer data, sending wrong invoices, or breaking a system your business depends on — professional help is insurance.
  • You don't know what you don't know. If you can't even articulate what AI should do for your business, a discovery session with a consultant can save you months of wandering.

The honest test: Can you clearly describe the problem in one sentence and name the tool that might solve it? If yes, try it yourself first. If you're stuck at "AI could help with... stuff?" — that's when a consultant earns their fee.

What does a good AI consultant actually do?

A good AI consultant doesn't just build things. They:

  1. Save you from expensive mistakes. They've seen what works and what doesn't across dozens of businesses. They know which tools are reliable and which are overhyped.
  2. Translate between business and tech. They turn "I'm drowning in admin" into a specific, buildable solution with clear costs and timelines.
  3. Build it to last. They create systems you can maintain yourself, not systems that require them to stick around forever (that's a bad consultant).
  4. Tell you no. The best consultants will tell you when AI isn't the answer. If someone's never talked a client out of a project, they're a salesperson, not a consultant.

How much should you expect to pay in Australia?

Typical rates for AI consulting in Australia:

  • Discovery session: $500 - $2,000 (a one-off assessment of your needs and a recommendation)
  • Hourly advisory: $150 - $350/hour
  • Project-based work: $2,000 - $20,000 for small business implementations

If someone quotes significantly less, ask why. If someone quotes significantly more, ask what you're getting that justifies it. Good consultants are transparent about pricing and scope.

Red flags in AI consultants

  • They promise AI will "revolutionise" your business (it won't — it'll improve specific processes)
  • They can't explain what they'll build in plain English
  • They push proprietary solutions that lock you in
  • They never say "you don't need AI for that"
  • They talk more about technology than about your business
