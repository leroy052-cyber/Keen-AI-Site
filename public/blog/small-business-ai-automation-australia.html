A Small Business Owner's Honest Guide to AI Automation

You've got 47 browser tabs open, at least three half-finished spreadsheets, and a vague sense that AI could help with... something. You're not wrong. But the internet is full of hype and you need someone to cut through it. Here goes.

Where should a small business start with AI?

Start with what annoys you most. Seriously. The best place to begin with AI automation isn't some grand "digital transformation strategy" — it's the one task you do every single day that makes you think "there has to be a better way."

For most Australian small businesses, that's one of these:

  • Email triage: Sorting through 50+ emails a day, most of which need the same basic response
  • Invoice processing: Manually entering data from supplier invoices into your accounting system
  • Appointment scheduling: The back-and-forth of "does Tuesday work? No? How about Thursday?"
  • Quoting: Pulling together quotes that follow the same basic template every time
  • Data entry: Any repetitive copying of information from one system to another

The rule of thumb: If you do a task more than 3 times a week, it follows roughly the same pattern each time, and it takes more than 10 minutes — it's a candidate for automation.

How much does AI automation cost in Australia?

Straight numbers, because you deserve them:

  • Simple automations (email handling, scheduling, basic workflows): $1,500 - $5,000 to set up, $50-100/month ongoing
  • Medium complexity (multi-system integrations, custom AI workflows): $5,000 - $15,000 to set up, $100-300/month ongoing
  • Complex implementations (custom AI models, enterprise integrations): $15,000+ to set up, $300-500/month ongoing

Most small businesses fall into the first two categories. And most see ROI within 2-4 months just from time savings — before counting the value of fewer errors, faster responses, and not losing invoices down the back of the couch.

What tasks can AI actually automate?

Here's a realistic list for small businesses in Australia — not the sci-fi version, the practical version:

  1. Email drafting and response: AI reads incoming emails and drafts responses for you to review and send. Works especially well for common enquiries.
  2. Invoice generation: Automatically create and send invoices when a job is completed or a product is shipped.
  3. Appointment booking: AI-powered scheduling that handles availability, reminders, and follow-ups.
  4. Customer enquiry handling: Chatbots and automated responses for frequently asked questions, available 24/7.
  5. Data processing: Extracting information from documents, forms, and emails into your systems automatically.
  6. Report generation: Automated weekly or monthly reports pulled from your business data.
  7. Social media scheduling: AI-assisted content creation and scheduling across platforms.

How to tell if you actually need AI

Here's the honest framework. You probably need AI automation if:

  • You're spending more than 10 hours a week on admin tasks that follow a pattern
  • You're losing money because things fall through the cracks (missed follow-ups, late invoices)
  • You're turning down work because you can't handle the admin that comes with it
  • Your team is doing data entry instead of the work they were hired to do

You probably don't need AI if:

  • Your admin load is manageable and you're not dropping balls
  • The "problem" is really just a missing spreadsheet or a shared calendar
  • You're looking for AI because it sounds cool, not because you have a specific problem to solve

A good AI consultant will tell you honestly which camp you fall in. If someone's pushing AI when you just need a better process, find a different consultant.
